# Foundation curriculum

Stable course anchors and modules; revise deliberately when a better course or a missing foundation is identified.

These are course-anchored editorial modules. Source-backed topics, optional extensions and atlas supplements are distinguished in their records. A module mapping does not claim that the atlas reproduces the entire course.

## Mathematics: From High School to Robotics Research

A branching orientation rather than a claim that one university course covers the entire path. Each learner can enter at an already understood concept; course membership is not proof of mastery. Algebra/probability, geometry, analysis, and computation develop partly in parallel.

- [OpenStax College Algebra 2e](https://openstax.org/details/books/college-algebra-2e)
- [Discrete Mathematics: An Open Introduction, author-hosted textbook](https://discrete.openmathbooks.org/dmoi3/frontmatter.html)
- [Stanford Math51 official topic sequence, Autumn2026](https://web.stanford.edu/class/math51/schedule.html)
- [Berkeley CS70 official topic list, Fall2026](https://www.eecs70.org/policies/)
- [Michigan ROB501 official educational overview](https://grizzle.robotics.umich.edu/education/rob501)
- [Stanford CS205L official numerical mathematics topics](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs205l/lectures.html)
- [Stanford EE364A: Convex Optimization I, official course](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/index.html)

Modules: Mathematical Language and School Algebra; Vectors, Geometry and Linear Maps; Rates, Accumulation and Local Models; Probability and Statistical Evidence; Projection and Spectral Structure; Optimization and Guarantees; Research Connections.

## Deep Reinforcement Learning

Course-aligned foundations in imitation, policy/value learning, inference, model-based and offline RL, exploration and multi-task decision making. The official prerequisite is CS189 or equivalent plus familiarity with RL, optimization and ML. Module grouping is editorial; contemporary LLM-RL material is linked as an advanced bridge rather than treated as a prerequisite for robotics.

## Deep Learning for Computer Vision

Official Spring 2026 emphasis is deep learning for visual recognition, with later material on video, self-supervision, generation, 3D vision, vision-language and world modeling. Python, calculus/linear algebra and basic probability are prerequisites. It is not represented as a complete classical multiview-geometry, SLAM or robotics course; those are linked foundations or research branches.

## Modern Robotics: Mechanics, Planning, and Control

Core robot mechanics and motion methods, anchored to the actual book chapters. Modern Robotics does not comprehensively cover probabilistic estimation, advanced legged/robust/optimal control, modern reinforcement learning or robot-learning deployment engineering. Supplementary node links extend the foundations; they are not claims that every linked algorithm is taught in the book.

- [Modern Robotics: official book, software and chapter outline](https://hades.mech.northwestern.edu/index.php/Modern_Robotics)

## Underactuated Robotics and Optimal Control

Advanced continuation after mechanics and basic feedback. Uses MIT notes for underactuation, stability, dynamic programming, trajectory optimization, contact and legged control. This is not an introductory substitute for calculus, linear algebra or mechanics.

- [MIT Underactuated Robotics notes](https://underactuated.mit.edu/)
- [MIT OCW Underactuated Robotics](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-832-underactuated-robotics-spring-2009/)

Modules: Robot State, Rollouts and Planning Interfaces; Underactuation and Hybrid Model Systems; Linear Feedback and Stability; Dynamic Programming and Trajectory Optimization; Legged and Contact-Rich Control; Robustness and Model Uncertainty.

## Visual Navigation, State Estimation and Mapping

Estimation complements Modern Robotics with probabilistic sensor fusion, visual-inertial odometry and sparse SLAM. The atlas offers conceptual foundations and primary course links; it does not claim a full implementation of every VIO or SLAM algorithm.

- [MIT Visual Navigation: public lecture materials](https://vnav.mit.edu/lectures.html)
- [MIT OCW 6.003: Signals and Systems](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-003-signals-and-systems-fall-2011/)

Modules: Signals, Sampling and Timing; Sensor Geometry and Calibration; Recursive State Estimation; Odometry, Smoothing and SLAM; Mapping and Navigation Interfaces; Object Measurements, Association and Tracking.

## Robot Simulation and Sim-to-Real Foundations

A dedicated introductory simulation track, distinct from CSE598 research readings. Learn asset compilation, integration, contacts, sensing, control stepping, environment semantics and validation before comparing engines or training speed. Drake, MuJoCo and Isaac Lab are complementary primary resources, not interchangeable claims about one course.

## Robotics Software and Research Engineering

Practical complement to theory and research seminars. Covers native software, ROS interfaces, data provenance, tests and runtime behavior. Tool documentation supports implementation; no single listed source is asserted to teach every module. Real hardware integration additionally requires the exact platform manual and measured system behavior. Parallel computing and training-system modules use CS149 for hardware/software principles and official CUDA/PyTorch documentation for implementation semantics; this does not claim CS149 teaches every PyTorch or distributed-training API.
